SOMA: A Benchmark for Fine-Grained Egocentric Behavior and Body-Motion Understanding
相关度 ★★★★★ (0.73)

作者：Matea Tashkovska, Na\"el Ouerghemi, Haozhe Qi, Alexander Mathis

发布日期：Wed,

类别：cs.CV

摘要

arXiv:2610.36384v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Perceiving human body motion is critical for multimodal systems that understand and assist people. Yet current evaluations of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) largely focus on objects, scenes, and coarse actions, leaving their ability to reason about the body itself poorly assessed. This is especially challenging from the egocentric perspective, in which the body is only partially visible and motion must be inferred from occluded and temporally distributed cues. We introduce SOMA, a benchmark for fine-grained body-motion understanding from egocentric video, comprising 1,632 human-verified questions across three capability axes: body configuration, motion dynamics, and temporal reasoning. We evaluate 37 leading closed-source, open-source, and specialized egocentric MLLMs, complemented by an extensive human evaluation. We find that humans also find this task difficult from video alone (68.5% accuracy), yet clearly outperform the strongest MLLM (Qwen3.6-27B), which reaches 50.0% zero-shot accuracy. Our error analysis shows that MLLMs often misread the wearer's own body, misjudge events in time, and are prone to biases such as favoring the right hand or under-counting repetitions. To narrow the gap to human performance and support further progress, we introduce a training dataset of aligned video, body motion, and language supervision, and a multimodal baseline that jointly learns from these modalities. By identifying common failure modes and missing capabilities in current MLLMs, we hope SOMA will inspire future research on body-motion understanding and multimodal reasoning. Full benchmark, training data, and code will be released at: https://cnai.epfl.ch/soma

Cross-modal Steering Vectors Exist Across Both Image and Text Generation
相关度 ★★★★☆ (0.50)

作者：Donghao Zeng (Mila, McGill University), Nishant Subramani (Carnegie Mellon University), Tamar Rott Shaham (MIT CSAIL), Benno Krojer (Mila, McGill University, Northeastern University)

发布日期：Wed,

类别：cs.CV

摘要

arXiv:2610.36269v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Are concepts and mechanisms unified across modalities in current AI models, or instead implemented separately for each one? We operationalize this broad question of modality unification by studying cross-modal steering: 1) finding vectors that successfully steer generation on tokens of one modality, which then 2) transfer zero-shot to steer generation when added to tokens of another modality. Our study goes beyond simple correlational alignment (e.g. representational similarity) because steering is fundamentally causal. We study cross-modal steering in several unified multimodal models (UMMs), since they possess shared parameters for language and image generation, focusing on these two modalities. Across diverse steered concepts such as emotion and object size, we show that steering vectors transfer zero-shot in both directions (text-to-image & image-to-text). Even for concepts that are highly unique to one modality (e.g. formal vs. colloquial language), steering vectors often induce a consistent, interpretable effect in the other modality, such as formal clothing and design. This transfer has a geometric signature too: despite a clear modality gap separating text and image representations, steering vectors with strong cross-modal transferability tend to point in similar directions for the same concept. Our findings shed light on how vision and language interact inside unified multimodal models and point towards more general, modality-agnostic approaches for controlling their behavior.

OA-RAG: Ontology-based Agentic RAG for Faithful LLM Reasoning
相关度 ★★★★☆ (0.49)

作者：Hui Yang, Jiaoyan Chen, Yongsheng Gao

发布日期：Wed,

类别：cs.AI

摘要

arXiv:2610.36335v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) perform well across many real-world tasks but remain prone to hallucination, limiting their reliability in domains such as biomedicine and healthcare, where answers must be grounded in established knowledge. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) mitigates this problem by incorporating external information, but often overlooks logical relationships and relies heavily on the LLM's own reasoning, which can compromise answer faithfulness. We propose OA-RAG, an agent that integrates formal reasoning with LLM reasoning, drawing on both the logical structure and informal annotations of domain ontologies. For each answer, OA-RAG reports the ontology observations used as evidence and a model-assessed grounding level (entailed, supported, related, or unrelated) that characterises the strength of that evidence. Across benchmarks derived from three large-scale ontologies and six real-world medical question-answering tasks, OA-RAG achieves the best overall performance among the evaluated ontology-fine-tuned and popular RAG methods, with particularly strong results on reasoning-intensive tasks. The reported grounding levels also offer a useful indication of answer reliability, with stronger grounding generally associated with higher accuracy. Finally, our results demonstrate the value of ontologies as knowledge sources: OA-RAG is competitive with, and sometimes outperforms, domain-specific RAG systems that draw on sources such as textbooks.

Learning to Read Braille: Benchmarking and Improving Braille Recognition Capabilities of VLMs
相关度 ★★★★☆ (0.48)

作者：Kartik Jangra, Atharva Gawane, Siddharth Pulipaka, Hemachandran Karah, Raj Dabre

发布日期：Wed,

类别：cs.CV

摘要

arXiv:2610.36195v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Despite rapid progress in visual understanding and document transcription, modern vision-language models (VLMs) struggle with a fundamental accessibility task: reading Braille from images. We introduce BrailleBench, a manually curated benchmark of 259 real-world Grade-1 and Grade-2 Braille images. We evaluate proprietary, open-weight, OCR-specialized, and tool-assisted multimodal systems and find that standalone general-purpose models perform poorly at directly transcribing Braille images, whereas tool-assisted systems perform much better. We design a synthetic Braille generation pipeline with diverse layouts and image perturbations. A 2B VLM model trained on our synthetic data reaches 87% exact match on Grade-1 and 61% on Grade-2 BrailleBench, outperforming standalone VLMs and achieving the highest Grade-2 exact match score among the evaluated systems. We further show that high accuracy on synthetic validation data does not guarantee high accuracy on real photographs in our experiments: models trained on prior synthetic data reach up to 96% on their synthetic validation set but at most 3.26% on Grade-1 BrailleBench. Our results identify an accessibility-relevant capability gap in multimodal systems and show that targeted synthetic supervision can improve real-world Braille transcription.

Across 300 pairs, four annotators agree with 88.42% of its sampled KEEP labels, against 26.27% for SPY-style augmentation of the same sources. Classifiers trained on it over-mask less than under that reference on a matched test. Contextual masking fine-tuned jointly with a PII detector removes most over-masking of detection alone within a fixed leakage budget; on human-verified entity records, the four detectors meeting it over-mask at most 5.04% with at most 4.18% leakage. The detector is comparable to XLM-R-large on KDPII with about a quarter of its parameters.

摘要

arXiv:2610.36249v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Dyadic relationship recognition aims to identify the latent social relationship between two individuals from their multimodal interactions. Existing methods typically project heterogeneous cues into a shared representation and optimise a flat classification objective, overlooking that relationships may rely on different interaction patterns. For instance, distant relationships can often be inferred from direct behavioural correspondence, whereas close relationships are better reflected by coordinated affective, behavioural, and multimodal responses. To address this limitation, we propose HiRe-DRR, a Hierarchical Multimodal Interaction Reasoning framework that organises relationship evidence from three complementary perspectives: feature level, interaction type, and relationship hierarchy. HiRe-DRR decomposes facial, bodily, acoustic, and textual cues into low- and high-level representations and constructs homogeneous and heterogeneous cross-participant interactions. We further introduce the Mixture-of-Interaction Transformer (MoI-Former), which treats diverse interactions as specialised experts and employs relation-aware sparse routing to identify relationship-relevant evidence while preserving contextual interaction information. The proposed Hierarchical Conditional Loss (HC-Loss) exploits the hierarchical nature of social relationships through coarse-to-fine supervision, enabling structured relationship reasoning. Extensive experiments on three dyadic relationship benchmarks demonstrate that HiRe-DRR outperforms recent representative image-, video-, and multimodal methods. Our routing analysis further reveals that distant relationships tend to rely on low-level homogeneous interactions, whereas closer relationships focus on high-level heterogeneous interactions. Our code is publicly available at: https://github.com/tw-repository/HiRe-DRR.
