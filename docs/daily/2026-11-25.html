Learning What vs. Learning How: Decomposing Task and Interface Competence in Agentic Training
相关度 ★★★★★ (0.64)

作者：Chaeeun Lee, Hossein A. Rahmani, Aryo Pradipta Gema, Kexin Xu, Emine Yilmaz, Pasquale Minervini, T. Ian Simpson, Chen Jin, Amrutha Saseendran

发布日期：Wed,

类别：cs.AI

摘要

arXiv:2611.31427v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: LLM agents operate through harnesses that define system prompts, action formats, feedback loops, and resource constraints. A model may possess the knowledge needed to solve a task yet fail to express that knowledge through a particular harness. This raises a basic question: when a policy fails under a given harness, is it missing task knowledge or the competence to operate the interface? We distinguish two components of agentic competence: task competence, the knowledge and reasoning needed to solve a task, and interface competence, the ability to act on that knowledge through a given harness. Across four reasoning and agentic benchmarks, we show that harness-conditioned training yields vastly different outcomes depending on how these interact. Multi-harness distillation aggregates task knowledge from trajectories collected through different harnesses into a shared policy, but this knowledge is realised only when training preserves or improves competence on the inference-time interface. Otherwise, single-harness training can induce interference in interface competence, sometimes catastrophically collapsing performance on a held-out harness despite improvements in underlying task knowledge. To disentangle task and interface supervision, we introduce harness rehearsal: distillation on traces that carry minimal task knowledge while exercising the target interface. Cross-harness task supervision or rehearsal alone yields little gain, but their combination yields gains far larger than either: on BFCL V3 multi-turn with Qwen3-4B, they achieve 8.5% and 32.5% respectively, versus 58.8% together. Finally, on-policy reinforcement learning (RL) is more robust than distillation to single-harness training: it does not substantially degrade performance on other harnesses, and interface competence acquired through multi-harness distillation persists through subsequent RL.

AI 总结

研究问题：区分LLM智能体在特定接口下失败的原因是缺乏任务知识还是接口操作能力。 主要方法：提出任务能力和接口能力两个概念，通过多接口蒸馏训练聚合知识，并分析不同训练方式的影响。 关键贡献：揭示了接口能力与任务能力的相互作用，以及单接口训练可能导致接口能力干扰甚至崩溃。

However, existing benchmarks largely focus on coarse-grained emotion recognition or binary truth-deception detection, overlooking the diverse ways people manipulate information in conversations, such as withholding, leakage, fabrication, evasion, and defensive counterclaim. Understanding these behaviors requires reasoning about underlying facts, participant knowledge, and conversational context, making them difficult to evaluate reliably. To address this gap, we introduce InfoCon, a multimodal benchmark for fine-grained information manipulation in conversations. SpatialCraft: Evolving Procedural Intelligence from Experience and Skills for Spatial Reasoning Agents
相关度 ★★★★★ (0.50)

作者：Xiwei Liu, Yulong Li, Xuhui Li, Zhixiang Lu, Haolin Yang, Jianxu Chen, Yifan Lu, Di Fan, Yutong Xie

发布日期：Wed,

类别：cs.AI

摘要

arXiv:2611.31475v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Spatial reasoning agents increasingly rely on external tools, such as 3D reconstruction, segmentation, and geometric computation, to answer questions that a multimodal large language model (MLLM) cannot resolve from pixels alone. Tool access, however, shifts the bottleneck to orchestration: the agent must decide which tools to call, in what order, and how to interpret their outputs, yet a stateless agent repeats the same mistakes across tasks. We ask whether a frozen tool-using agent can improve its orchestration from its own interaction history, without parameter updates. We present SpatialCraft, a self-evolving framework that maintains two forms of external knowledge: Experiences, condition-action tactics distilled by contrasting rollouts of the same task, and Skills, multi-step procedures with explicit activation and termination conditions. Three designs couple the two streams. Experiences can be anchored to a Skill, appearing only while it is active rather than once per task. A Contrastive Utility Score estimates each Experience's effect by randomly withholding it across rollouts of the same task, separating its contribution from task difficulty, and Experiences with consistently positive utility are consolidated into the Skills they support. Across five spatial benchmarks and four backbones, SpatialCraft achieves the best accuracy in all 20 settings, showing that a frozen agent can learn to orchestrate spatial tools from its own experience.

摘要

Each stage can use one of many candidate models. Estimating end-to-end workflow quality by deploying and evaluating every resulting model configuration is therefore prohibitively expensive. As a result, existing approaches face a tradeoff between estimation cost and accuracy: inexpensive methods often produce unreliable quality estimates, while accurate methods require deploying and evaluating many workflow configurations. In this paper, we introduce DAGGER, a framework for estimating the quality of generative AI workflows without exhaustive deployment and profiling. DAGGER represents a workflow as a graph, in which nodes correspond to stages and edges capture how outputs from one stage affect subsequent stages. It then decomposes workflow quality into per-node accuracy terms and per-edge dependency terms. Using a novel analytical formulation and SVD-based matrix completion, DAGGER estimates workflow quality from sparse observations without executing every candidate workflow. We evaluate DAGGER on three generative AI workflows across three datasets. DAGGER estimates workflow quality with a median error of 6.8 percentage points while reducing profiling cost by 10.2$\times$ relative to BenchPress, a state-of-the-art baseline, and selects workflow configurations competitive with state-of-the-art frameworks such as LLMSelector.

摘要

arXiv:2611.31571v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Autoformalization translates a natural-language statement into a formula that captures its intent, which depends on the statement's context: the background that disambiguates it. LLMs make autoformalization practical, but their probabilistic output carries no formal guarantees. We propose techniques for safe autoformalization that certify one-sided logical adequacy: a formula sufficient for the intent is safe to enforce, and a formula necessary for it is safe to rely on. We use LLMs to generate candidate formulas and order them by logical strength in an Entailment Graph (EG), an ordering a solver decides. Each edge carries a residual and a co-residual, the two adjoints of the entailment order, all fixed by the candidate pool alone. In a context, two searches return the sufficient frontier, the weakest candidates that entail the statement, and the necessary frontier, the strongest candidates the statement entails; each step is one local check against an edge's residual or co-residual. The frontiers bracket the statement and yield the ambiguity residue, which holds exactly of the situations the candidates certify neither as satisfying nor as violating it; the statement is ambiguous precisely when the residue is satisfiable. For natural-language statements and contexts, an LLM entailment oracle decides the checks; it is sound if it accepts only checks that hold, and complete if it accepts every check that holds. We prove that a sound oracle makes the search safe regardless of pool quality, and that a complete one makes the frontiers tight. On security-critical AWS properties, EGs are safe in all 20 cases of an expert-adjudicated study, where majority vote over equivalence clusters and Universal Self-Consistency are unsafe in 25% and 10%. An ablation with the same oracle but no graph is unsafe in 25% and rejects every candidate in another 30%. We machine-checked our proofs in Lean.

研究问题：如何确保自动形式化中公式的逻辑充分性和必要性，提供形式保证。 主要方法：用LLM生成候选公式，通过蕴含图排序，利用残差和协残差进行局部检查，得到充分前沿和必要前沿。 关键贡献：提出安全自动形式化框架，通过蕴含图界定语句，计算歧义残差，实现逻辑充分性和必要性的形式认证。
