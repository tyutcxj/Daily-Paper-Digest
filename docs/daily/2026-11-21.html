Some Memories Must Fade: Persistence and Decay of User Memories in Model Parameters
相关度 ★★★★★ (0.60)

作者：Sanidhya Vijayvargiya, Rahul Lokesh

发布日期：Sat,

类别：cs.LG

摘要

arXiv:2611.25235v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Parametric memory allows Large Language Models to internalize knowledge directly into their weights, offering a potentially powerful alternative to retrieval-based memory (RAG). However, existing approaches to parametric memory are difficult to deploy as they largely mirror the Continual Learning (CL) objective of retaining information indefinitely. This is neither sustainable nor sufficient for a memory system, which must govern knowledge across its lifecycle by retaining stable facts, reinforcing recurring observations, updating changed beliefs, and allowing transient information to fade. We propose a system for parametric memory management consisting of a memory substrate (handling information storage and forgetting) and a learned policy (what to remember, how deeply, and for how long). Our substrate is a fixed-capacity LoRA adapter that consists of k-hot rank combinations which store facts without catastrophic interference and maintain an independently controlled temporal decay, enabling outdated information to fade while long-term facts persist. On this substrate, we train a lightweight controller that governs each incoming user event to ensure the memory system reflects the user's true, updated belief state. To evaluate how memory systems manage user memory, we introduce a controlled dataset of long-horizon user trajectories with varying persistence of facts based on changing belief states and user-behavioral shifts, and probe queries that test recall while information is valid and abstention after it expires. Our policy learns to effectively manage user memory over its entire lifecycle, balancing recall and abstention to achieve 86% of oracle performance, unlike CL and RAG baselines that primarily default to one of the two behaviors. By providing the first system to manage memory directly in model weights, this work establishes a foundation for long-horizon parametric memory.

Moving Generality from Model to Modeler: LLM Agents for PHM on a Deployment-Style Benchmark
相关度 ★★★★★ (0.51)

作者：Zhenling Chen, Zhiguo Zeng, Anne Barros

发布日期：Sat,

类别：cs.LG

摘要

arXiv:2611.25252v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Coding agents built on large language models (LLMs) now help develop many models published as general solutions. Should generality instead reside in the agent that builds a model for each new task? We examine this question in prognostics and health management (PHM), where every model must work on unseen equipment. Our deployment-style benchmark, NextUnit-Bench, covers 24 public datasets split into 58 folds that hold out units or operating conditions; each agent has two hours to deliver an executable predictor, then run on the hidden units. First, frontier agents outperform AutoML, which reaches less than half the best agent's score, and faithful implementations of published methods on four datasets, of which only 6 of 30 beat the strongest agent's own design; published accuracies, where reproducible, rest on the papers' own conventions, and a single convention, capping the remaining life of test engines, can halve the reported error. Second, the agents' advantage is a design made on each machine's data; once made, a plain-text plan of a few hundred words carries most of the gap between two frontier agents, and given a weaker agent's plan, the strongest agent falls to that agent's level, often to identical predictions. Before touching the data, however, it matters little whose plan an agent carries out, and domain toolkits appear to help neither: what sets the strongest apart is how much it learns from the data. Third, such designs reach only implementers that can execute them: given the strongest agent's designs, a locally deployable open-weight model delivers half as often, even when they are lightened, and scores no higher than without them. Generality, we argue, is not disappearing but moving, from the model to the modeler.

Rate-distortion Assessment for Hierarchical Content Summarization
相关度 ★★★★☆ (0.45)

作者：Yunye Gong, Indranil Sur, Jared Claypoole, Pritish Sahu, Ajay Divakaran

发布日期：Sat,

类别：cs.LG

摘要

arXiv:2611.25151v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Powerful generative models including large language and vision models have demonstrated success across a wide range of content generation, editing and manipulation tasks via end-to-end prompt-based generation. However, current assessment of the quality of generative content manipulation remains subjective, limiting scientific understanding of model capabilities. Inspired by rate-distortion theory from Shannon information compression theory, in this work we formalize hierarchical content summarization as an information encoding problem. We design a principled framework to extract the semantic hierarchy of the source content to support quantitative characterization of the rate-distortion trade-off between compression efficiency (rate) against information loss (distortion) during generative content manipulation, which allows us to benchmark various models with respect to performance conditional on rate budget of interest. We demonstrate the use of the framework with empirical experiments on content summarization for long-context question-answering with state-of-the-art large language models, offering new insights into their efficiency and comprehension capabilities. We further demonstrate the use of the semantic hierarchy as guidance for performing effective retrieval augmented generation, showing improved rate-distortion characteristics against current baselines.

HapBO: Harnessed $\alpha$-$\pi$ Bayesian Optimization via Expert Natural Language Priors
相关度 ★★★★☆ (0.43)

作者：Jean-S\'ebastien Giroux, Rose Guay Hottin, Marco Bonizzato

发布日期：Sat,

类别：cs.LG

摘要

arXiv:2611.25100v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Bayesian Optimization (BO) can be substantially more sample-efficient when informed by prior knowledge about the problem. In practice, however, such knowledge is often available only in natural language, e.g., expert intuitions or documentation, rather than as the explicit probability distribution landscape that prior-guided BO methods may require. Leveraging it effectively demands both an expressive mapping from language to priors and robustness to information that may be misleading. We propose HapBO (Harnessed $\alpha$-$\pi$ Bayesian Optimization), which elicits beliefs once from a human expert, a large language model (LLM), or both, and deterministically compiles a structured specification into a reusable belief function ($\pi: \mathcal{X} \to[0,1]$), with a rich representation not restricted to a Gaussian family. Building on $\alpha$-$\pi$BO, HapBO applies this belief function through a bounded positive multiplier on any nonnegative acquisition, preserving positive acquisition support and requiring no LLM calls during optimization. We prove that, for the Expected Improvement acquisition function (EI), HapBO preserves the standard simple-regret rate up to a bounded factor, even under adversarial beliefs. We evaluate HapBO on 40 HPOBench tasks, a synthetic suite in 6-15 dimensions and two real-world applications: cortical neurostimulation and constrained solar design. HapBO achieves the lowest aggregate HPOBench regret and strong real-world task performance, with zero in-loop token cost. In real-world applications it improves the optimization outcome even at budgets of 12-20 queries, and at a small fraction of the wall-clock and token costs of other LLM-based methods.

Separating Interpretability and Reconstruction in Sparse Autoencoders
相关度 ★★★☆☆ (0.37)

作者：Anh Tien Nguyen, Dimitris Samaras, Mahdi S. Hosseini

发布日期：Sat,

类别：cs.LG

摘要

arXiv:2611.25165v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) are effective tools for mechanistic interpretability by decomposing neural representations into sparse and interpretable features. However, a single sparse representation in traditional SAEs is responsible for both reconstruction fidelity and interpretability, which are not guaranteed to align. To separate these objectives, we propose DualSAE, a modular framework that augments standard SAEs with a plug-in Residual Branch. A standard SAE serves as the Sparse Branch, learning a sparse code that contains the dominant semantics of the input. The added Residual Branch models the non-semantic information omitted by the Sparse Branch. We ensure that the contributions of the two branches towards the overall reconstruction have minimal overlap through a decorrelation loss. We also adopt a two-stage training strategy that first stabilizes sparse representation learning before coupling with the Residual Branch. Across diverse encoder-SAE configurations on two vision datasets and two language corpora, DualSAE achieves mean relative improvements of approximately 29% in reconstruction fidelity and 34% in interpretability (measured by monosemanticity score). Moreover, with more interpretable representations, DualSAE yields consistent performance gains in the downstream applications of topic modeling, hypothesis generation, and LLM steering. Qualitative analysis further suggests that the Sparse Branch indeed preserves dominant semantic content, whereas the Residual Branch captures non-semantic information to improve reconstruction fidelity.
