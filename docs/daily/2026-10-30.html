Where Can We Fool the Model's Mind? Response-Guided Semantic Probing and Adaptive Routing for Transferable Attacks on Black-Box MLLMs
相关度 ★★★★★ (0.56)

作者：Ahmed Abdelnaby, Mohamed Elmahallawy

发布日期：Fri,

类别：cs.CV

摘要

arXiv:2610.38013v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) ground language in visual evidence, yet their semantic reasoning can be exploited through transferable black-box adversarial attacks. Existing attacks typically allocate perturbations using surrogate-model saliency, which may not reflect how an unseen MLLM semantically interprets or relies on different image regions. We introduce VISTA (Vulnerability-Informed Semantic Transfer Attacks), a black-box framework that identifies semantically vulnerable visual regions from an MLLM's responses without access to gradients, logits, parameters, or internal representations. VISTA supports two attack settings: (i) VISTA-Proxy, a pure-transfer setting that probes an open-source MLLM and transfers the resulting adversarial examples to unseen commercial MLLMs without querying them; and (ii) VISTA-Victim, a query-assisted setting that probes the target MLLM to capture model-specific semantic vulnerabilities. VISTA builds an Uncertainty-Based Multimodal Behavior (UBMB) map from response drift, conflict, misleading, and semantic shifts, then uses entropy-adaptive Sinkhorn routing and a surrogate ensemble to focus perturbations on vulnerable regions. Across GPT-5, Gemini-3.5-Flash, and Claude Sonnet 4.6, VISTA achieves up to 97 % attack success rate (ASR) while maintaining a Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) above 30 dB. Notably, VISTA-Proxy achieves up to 93 % ASR without querying the final victim, demonstrating that response-derived semantic vulnerability can enable effective transfer across heterogeneous MLLMs.

AI 总结

研究问题：黑盒MLLM的可迁移对抗攻击中，现有方法依赖代理模型显著性，无法反映目标模型语义解释。 主要方法：提出VISTA框架，通过响应不确定性构建UBMB图，采用熵自适应Sinkhorn路由和代理集成聚焦扰动于脆弱区域。 关键贡献：无需梯度/参数，通过响应探测实现纯迁移或查询辅助攻击，在多个商业MLLM上取得高攻击成功率。

SkillMem: Compact External Skill Memory for Latent Multi-Agent Reasoning
相关度 ★★★★★ (0.51)

作者：Yuxuan Zhu, Mohammad Mohammadi Amiri, Pin-Yu Chen, Keerthiram Murugesan

发布日期：Fri,

类别：cs.AI

摘要

arXiv:2610.37935v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Latent multi-agent systems reduce inference cost by keeping intermediate reasoning and inter-agent communication in continuous representations.\ Reusable skills, however, are commonly supplied as text or stored as layer-wise Key-Value states or skill-specific model parameters, introducing repeated processing or substantial per-skill storage.\ We introduce \textbf{SkillMem}, which represents each reusable skill as a small external latent memory and makes it directly available during latent multi-agent reasoning.\ A role-conditioned cross-attention reader allows the same memory to guide different agents according to their roles and current hidden states.\ We train the skill memories and shared reader through behavioral distillation, matching the change in token predictions induced by a textual skill while keeping the backbone frozen.\ For our experiments, we obtain textual skills from paired successful and failed multi-agent trajectories, although existing skills can also be compiled directly.\ Across six benchmarks and Qwen3 models ranging from 4B to 14B parameters, SkillMem provides strong gains for smaller backbones while preserving efficient latent multi-agent inference across model scales.\ We use the same $8\times256$ memory configuration across all evaluated model scales, requiring 9.6\,KB of skill-specific storage per skill, including its retrieval embedding.\ For a library of 100 skills, SkillMem reduces total storage by more than $38\times$ compared with alternative skill representations and achieves up to a $4.23\times$ end-to-end inference speedup.\ These results show that compact procedural memories can be reused without sacrificing the efficiency of latent multi-agent reasoning.

研究问题：如何高效地在潜在多智能体推理中复用技能，避免重复处理和高存储成本。 主要方法：提出SkillMem，将技能表示为小型外部潜在记忆，通过角色条件交叉注意力读取器指导智能体，并用行为蒸馏训练。 关键贡献：在保持高效推理的同时，为小模型带来显著提升，且固定记忆配置跨模型规模有效。

SciGame: Let Agents Play the Scientific Discovery Games in the Real and Counterfactual World
相关度 ★★★★☆ (0.46)

作者：Yusheng Zhao, Bowei He, Xue Liu, Mingxiao Li

发布日期：Fri,

类别：cs.AI

摘要

arXiv:2610.37919v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Large language model agents are increasingly used for scientific discovery. However, existing benchmarks mix law inference with programming, score data fitting instead of prediction on unseen inputs, and do not separate empirical evidence from pretrained priors. We introduce SciGame, a controlled benchmark that converts scientific literature into structured scientific cards. Each card yields a pair of Original and dimensionally consistent Counterfactual environments with the same task interface. SciGame contains a Historical collection of canonical laws and a live Temporal collection curated from papers published after model knowledge cutoffs. It evaluates agents in candidate-guided Identification and open-ended Discovery settings. Each setting runs under Prior-only, Fixed, and Adaptive protocols. Experiments on five frontier models yield three findings. First, familiar priors hurt performance once the governing relation is altered. Second, adaptive experimentation helps the strongest models but harms weaker ones under limited budgets. Third, good explanations can hide quantitative failures. Recovering the exact symbolic form remains the main bottleneck, even when agents interpret evidence correctly. Current agents can investigate systems actively, but inducing exact laws in unfamiliar settings remains an open challenge.

AI 总结

研究问题：现有科学发现基准混淆定律推断与编程，未区分经验证据与预训练先验。 主要方法：提出SciGame基准，将文献转化为结构化卡片，提供原始和反事实环境，评估agent在识别和发现任务中的表现。 关键贡献：揭示熟悉先验在关系改变时有害、自适应实验对弱模型不利、解释可能掩盖定量失败，精确符号恢复仍是瓶颈。

An Introduction to Large Language Models for Scientists and Professionals
相关度 ★★★★☆ (0.45)

作者：Umberto Michelucci

发布日期：Fri,

类别：cs.CL

摘要

arXiv:2610.37684v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Large Language Models (LLMs) have rapidly become important tools for science, education, medicine, and professional work. Despite their apparent simplicity from the user's perspective, effective and responsible use requires an understanding of how these systems work, what their limitations are, and how their outputs should be evaluated. I wrote this document as a basis for my lecture on LLM in the master in artificial intelligence in medicine at the university of Turin, Italy. This book provides a practical and accessible introduction to LLMs for scientists and professionals. It explains the fundamental ideas behind tokenisation, embeddings, training, inference, context windows, prompting, and instruction hierarchies, while deliberately avoiding mathematical detail. Particular attention is given to the limitations of LLM-generated answers, including hallucinations, uncertainty, reproducibility, bias, and the need for systematic verification and human oversight. The book also discusses privacy, governance, security, and the responsible handling of personal and sensitive information. More advanced topics include Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), retrieval quality, chunking, embeddings, vector search, agents, tools, and workflows. A substantial practical exercise guides the reader through running a local LLM with Ollama and Open WebUI and using it to interact with a collection of scientific papers. The exercise emphasizes evidence retrieval, source verification, uncertainty, disagreement between studies, and the distinction between plausible language and scientifically supported conclusions.
