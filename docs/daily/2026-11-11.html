Multimodal Graph Learning: A Survey of Models, Benchmarks, and Frontiers
相关度 ★★★★★ (0.54)

作者：Rui Xue, Tianfu Wu

发布日期：Wed,

类别：cs.LG

摘要

arXiv:2611.12990v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Multimodal attributed graphs (MAGs), in which nodes carry text, images, and other modalities while edges encode relations among entities, underlie e-commerce, social media, content platforms, and creative applications. Learning on MAGs has developed along two complementary lines: graph-centric methods redesign how modalities are fused, propagated, and connected within message passing, while foundation-model-centric methods pretrain transferable multimodal graph encoders or bring graph context into multimodal large language models for prediction and generation. In parallel, several benchmarks have appeared with different datasets and evaluation protocols. This survey takes an algorithm-centric and evaluation-grounded view of the field and makes three contributions. Modeling: we introduce a unified message-passing formulation and organize methods into five integration levels (feature, propagation, topology, learning, and foundation model), covering discriminative and graph-conditioned generative models; within this formulation we derive representative methods as special cases, compare their assumptions, expressiveness, and cost, and identify cross-modal sensitivity as a property that separates otherwise similar architectures. Datasets and evaluation: we review recent benchmarks, summarize their datasets and metrics, define model-independent descriptors (label homophily, average degree, clustering, and edge provenance), and map each evaluation protocol to the baselines it uses. Frontiers: we connect multimodal graph learning to AI agents, covering the multimodal graphs that agents perceive and remember and graph engineering, the practice of organizing agent workflows as explicit graphs; we then discuss open problems, concrete efforts for the community, and research directions, including when graph structure helps in the presence of strong multimodal foundation models.

Graph Visualizations in Prompts for Knowledge Graph Question Answering
相关度 ★★★★☆ (0.50)

作者：Luiz do Valle Miranda, Andreas L. Opdahl, Grzegorz J. Nalepa

发布日期：Wed,

类别：cs.AI

摘要

arXiv:2611.12938v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We study when and how multimodal input improves knowledge graph question answering (KGQA) in large vision--language models. Using a controlled evaluation over 1,500 WebQSP and CWQ questions and six VLMs within a fixed KGQA pipeline, we compare text-only prompting against text-plus-graph visualization at the per-question level and analyze performance changes as a function of reasoning complexity, model size, and image layout. Adding a graph image yields an average gain of 1.85 F1 points, driven by strong precision improvements on low-complexity questions (+4.65 F1). Mixed-effects and regression analyses show that these multimodal gains do not depend strongly on model scale, but are highly sensitive to graph layout: image-only settings sharply degrade performance, hierarchical Graphviz layouts outperform less organized renderings, and decomposing large subgraphs into separated paths yields the largest improvements. Taken together, the results indicate that visual prompting primarily aids KGQA by enhancing prediction precision in low-complexity contexts, provided that visual design respects cognitive and perceptual constraints. To optimize both accuracy and inference efficiency, we argue for adaptive visual prompting policies that selectively append diagrams to small, visually sparse subgraphs with well-structured layouts, avoiding unnecessary visual clutter and token overhead on complex queries. We conclude by relating these findings to a visual notation design theory, outlining how principled diagram encoding can guide future multimodal VLM prompting.

摘要

arXiv:2611.13028v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: AAA games offer a rich testbed for evaluating agent perception, reasoning, and control. However, evaluating whether agents can complete tasks in AAA games is challenging and often human-annotated because task outcomes are often difficult to verify automatically, let alone applying it for Reinforcement Learning (RL). We introduce GTAGym, an environment and framework for evaluation and RL on GTA V tasks. Its evaluation benchmark, GTAVBench, derived from GTA V main-story missions, contains 103 online tasks reconstructed across nine task families, alongside 47 image-based shooting and sniping tasks using GTA V imagery. A web server provides access to the online environment and evaluation interface, supporting parallel testing through screenshots and keyboard-mouse actions with automatic engine-state outcome checks. We evaluate six frontier LLMs on GTAVBench with textual task instructions and on its GTAVBench-AGI branch, where instructions appear only in the first screenshot and a harness automatically compresses interaction history. The best configuration achieves 70.9% overall accuracy in GTAVBench-AGI, yet aerial navigation, evasion and interception remain difficult. GTAGym also provides a scalable generator that samples task parameters within user-specified ranges and option sets under fixed templates, creating batches of 3D and image-based shooting environments for RL with automatic reward and termination signals. LoRA RL of Qwen3.8-27B on generated on-foot navigation tasks improves the in-domain performance and transfers to out-of-domain task categories, and native GTA V tasks from 15% to 23%.

Agent Architecture Cost-Success Trade-offs: A Controlled Evaluation across Enterprise-Style SOPs
相关度 ★★★★☆ (0.47)

作者：Subhrangshu Nandi, Hitesh Arora, Yunfei Bai, Udita Patel, Chirag Shah, Hannah Kwon, Wei Zhang, Weian Chen, Chloe Ruoqi Li, Alberto Sconfienza, Ayush Goyal, Yasmine Lee, Ming Xiong, Prathap Ramachandra

发布日期：Wed,

类别：cs.AI

摘要

arXiv:2611.12953v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Enterprises increasingly use large language model agents to execute standard operating procedures (SOPs): long, branching, tool-intensive workflows. Yet there is little controlled evidence for choosing among competing agentic architectures. Holding the model, tasks, and tools fixed and varying only the architecture, we compare four designs (a function-calling single agent, a skill-augmented agent, centralized multi-agent orchestration, and a triage swarm; eight arms) across 13 enterprise SOPs, and introduce a skill-definition quality rubric to build matched high and low-quality skill corpora. Three takeaways emerge.
