相关度 ★★★★★ (0.52)

作者：Yixiang Tang, Fagui Liu, Guoxiang Zhong, Xi Yao, C. L. Philip Chen

发布日期：Mon,

类别：cs.LG

摘要

arXiv:2610.31722v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Microservice systems have become a dominant architecture for large-scale cloud applications, enabling rapid deployment and elastic scaling. However, ensuring the reliability and availability of microservice systems critically relies on effective anomaly detection, as failures and performance anomalies can quickly propagate across services and severely impact end-to-end system behavior. Existing multimodal anomaly detection approaches for microservice systems often struggle with two fundamental limitations: (i) the neglect of modality-specific characteristics in favor of cross-modality fusion, and (ii) the "identity-shortcut" problem that affects the feature reconstruction process. To address these issues, we propose LLMMAD, an autoregressive multimodal anomaly detection framework for microservice systems based on Large Language Models (LLMs). Initially, to reconcile cross-modality fusion with modality-specific preservation, LLMMAD embeds and recombines multimodal data into a unified sequence for LLM processing to model inter-modality relationships, while employing modality-wise reconstruction heads to retain modality-specific information. Subsequently, to eliminate the identity-shortcut, we implement a prediction-driven autoregressive objective, under which the model predicts future states rather than copies current inputs. Finally, we conduct extensive experiments on three real-world datasets. The results demonstrate that LLMMAD consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods, achieving F1-score improvements of 3.1%, 1.4%, and 0.9%, respectively, thereby providing a more reliable solution for microservice monitoring.

作者：Yepeng Liu, Shengmai Chen, Xuandong Zhao, Chengzhi Liu, Dawn Song, Xin Eric Wang, Yuheng Bu

发布日期：Mon,

类别：cs.AI

摘要

arXiv:2610.32046v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Recent \emph{test-time training} (TTT) frameworks have enabled open-weight large language models (LLMs) to outperform inference-only frameworks powered by frontier models on scientific discovery tasks. However, TTT introduces additional computational cost for online weight updates during the discovery process, making computational efficiency an important consideration when scaling to larger models. This motivates us to explore an efficient \emph{test-time adaptation} method for scientific discovery. We introduce \emph{Test-Time Memory Evolution} (TTME), a plug-and-play test-time adaptation method, as an alternative to TTT. TTME continually updates the external memory by distilling the experience to guide subsequent discovery without updating model weights. Our evaluation shows that TTME outperforms TTT on all five mathematical tasks, while reducing computational cost by $82.8$-$84.6\%$ in FLOPs and $47.7$-$58.4\%$ in runtime. Benchmarks Are Surprisingly Compaction-Friendly
相关度 ★★★★☆ (0.49)

作者：Jeanne Salle, Hardik Bhatnagar, Matthias Bethge, Sahar Abdelnabi, Maksym Andriushchenko

发布日期：Mon,

类别：cs.LG

摘要

arXiv:2610.31778v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Long horizon agentic tasks are now pushing LLM agents past their context window. To continue the task, the agent scaffold has to free up the context window, a process known as context compaction. Compaction is generally lossy by design: agents can silently lose task constraints, instructions, or their reasoning behind earlier decisions. Although compaction is a standard component of coding agent scaffolds, it has not been studied systematically with frontier models on realistic, long horizon agentic tasks. Previous work evaluate smaller open weight models on toy or synthetic trajectories. We fix the agent scaffold (OpenCode), implement five compaction strategies inside it, and evaluate three frontier models on three long horizon benchmarks (PaperBench, ProgramBench, PostTrainBench). For the two 1M context models, the compaction threshold does not measurably affect the task performance, whereas GPT-5.3-Codex, the model with the smallest context window, performs worse at lower thresholds on PaperBench and, less clearly, on PostTrainBench. No compaction method outperforms default self-summarization, and methods that discard more content cost some models up to 20 points while leaving other models the same. Compaction can, however, silently remove task constraints: on ProgramBench, the first compaction summary already drops about one third of the task's anticheating rules on average, and this rises monotonically with the number of compactions. On real user-agent sessions from SWE-chat, instructions about how the agent should work and what it should not do are dropped around 6 times more than specifications of what to build. We argue that current benchmarks are compaction-friendly: i.e., the objective is fixed and the agent's progress stays on disk, so most of what compaction discards can be recovered, except constraints that don't exist anywhere else.

Phronix: The Capability-Centric Learning Paradigm for Agents
相关度 ★★★★☆ (0.47)

作者：Hariharan Devarajan, Neeraj Rajesh, Aniruddha Marathe, Nikoli Dryden, Kin Ng, Michela Taufer

发布日期：Mon,

类别：cs.AI

摘要

arXiv:2610.31789v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Scientific computing increasingly relies on large language model-based agents for performance engineering tasks such as instrumentation, analysis, and workflow porting. However, agent effectiveness depends heavily on the surrounding harness, which typically relies on manually written skills and curated memory. This limits autonomous learning, forcing agents to repeatedly rediscover solutions rather than retain and reuse knowledge from prior executions. We introduce Phronix, a capability-centric learning paradigm that enables agentic harnesses to acquire, validate, and refine reusable capabilities from their own executions. Phronix operates through a predictor-validator-corrector loop: MCP tools and associated skills propose solutions, tool executions validate them, and observed outcomes refine the stored capabilities. It distills every observed interaction into a capability store and researches a failing task rather than abandoning it. First, such learned capabilities solve a recurring task on the first attempt in 93-95% of cases across every model, significantly reducing solution non-determinism and improving reliability. Second, Phronix turns an agent's own execution into 7.6-30.8 durable capabilities per task without user instruction, and creation falls below two once the needed capability exists. Third, warm-start runs consume only 0.38-0.40 million tokens per task, which is three to four times less than a single baseline attempt, while a cold-start costs no more than the baseline. On the 1000 Genome Pegasus workflow, instrumenting a production application for I/O performance analysis, Phronix passes all 11 correctness checks against six for the baseline harness, and also lifts the task solving rates of two cheaper models from 29% and 27% of SWE-bench tasks solved to 91% and 87%, respectively, matching the performance of the most expensive evaluated model.

We observe that the benefit of additional modalities varies across queries, indicating that a fixed modality configuration can be inefficient. Motivated by this, we study query-wise modality selection, which keeps the underlying Omni-LLM fixed while adaptively selecting which modalities to provide for each query. We formulate the problem as an accuracy-cost optimization problem and develop a cost-sensitive learning approach for modality selection. Speculative Diffusion is a Tale of Two Tradeoffs
相关度 ★★★★☆ (0.47)

作者：Aaron Zorel, Guy Van den Broeck, Aditya Grover, Daniel Israel

发布日期：Mon,

类别：cs.LG

摘要

arXiv:2610.31819v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Speculative decoding is a technique that accelerates the inference speed of autoregressive large language models (LLMs) by sampling from a faster, lower quality draft model. Traditionally, a fundamental characteristic of speculative decoding is that it is lossless with respect to the verifier distribution, guaranteeing speed without sacrificing quality. This differs from recently developed diffusion LLMs (dLLMs), which are notably able to achieve substantial speedups by degrading the quality of their outputs with less diffusion effort. Because diffusion models are able to sample faster than autoregressive models by generating tokens in parallel, they are considered strong candidates for draft models in speculative diffusion. While it is widely understood that diffusion models can sacrifice quality for speed, speculative decoding itself can also be generalized to generate lossy samples faster using more lenient acceptance criteria with respect to the verifier distribution. Presented with the two parameters of diffusion effort and speculative decoding leniency, this paper explores the full achievable Pareto frontier via joint variations of these parameters. Our experiments show that jointly varying diffusion effort and acceptance leniency can extend the speed-quality Pareto frontier over single step diffusion drafting.
